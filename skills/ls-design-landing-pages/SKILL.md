# L.S.Design Landing Pages

Create a persuasive, specific narrative without turning every page into the same conversion template.

## Decision order

User and repository requirements come first, followed by product and brand truth, accessibility and function, this specialist's guidance, then aesthetic defaults.

## Workflow

1. Establish the audience, promise, proof, objections, conversion action, and traffic context.
2. Choose the narrative order from those facts; do not force a fixed acronym or section count.
3. Design the first viewport to communicate identity, value, and a credible next action.
4. Build proof with real product views, demonstrations, outcomes, comparisons, or testimonials appropriate to the claim.
5. Use section-level composition changes to pace the argument while preserving a common visual language.
6. Validate the page with real copy, narrow screens, slow loading, keyboard use, and the actual conversion path.

## Quality bar

- Prefer concrete product language over inflated claims and decorative statistics.
- Do not fabricate customers, awards, testimonials, metrics, or integrations.
- Make calls to action specific and consistent with what happens next.
- Keep the main action prominent without visually shouting in every section.
- Use motion and 3D to demonstrate value or establish atmosphere, never to delay comprehension.
- Ensure forms expose labels, errors, requirements, privacy context, and success states.
- Preserve campaign parameters and analytics behavior when redesigning an existing page.
